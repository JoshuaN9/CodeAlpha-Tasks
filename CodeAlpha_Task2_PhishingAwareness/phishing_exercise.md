# PHISHING IDENTIFICATION EXERCISE
# PURPOSE
This exercise tests the ability to recognize phishing indicators and make safe decisions when handling suspicious messages.

# Scenario 1: Suspicious Email
Subject: Urgent Account Verification Required
From: `security-alert@example.invalid`
## Message:
"Your account access will be disabled today. Verify your login details immediately to avoid losing access."

## Questions
1. Identify at least three warning signs.
2. What should the recipient do?
3. Should the recipient reply with their password?

# Scenario 2: Unexpected Delivery Message
## Message:
"Your package could not be delivered. Pay a small redelivery fee using the link provided."
The recipient was not expecting a package.

## Questions
1. What makes this message suspicious?
2. How can the recipient verify the delivery claim safely?
3. What should the recipient avoid doing?

# Scenario 3: Fake Workplace Request
# Message:
"Hello, I am your manager. I am in a meeting. Purchase gift cards immediately and send me the redemption codes. Do not call me."

## Questions
1. Which social-engineering techniques are being used?
2. How should the employee verify the request?
3. Why is the instruction not to call suspicious?

# SUGESTED ANSWERS
## Scenario 1
- Warning signs include urgency, a threat of account suspension, and a request to verify login details through an unexpected message.
- Visit the service using a trusted address or bookmark and report the message if suspicious.
- Never send a password in reply.

## Scenario 2
- The recipient was not expecting a package, and the message requests payment through an unexpected link.
- Check the delivery company's official website or contact channel independently.
- Do not click the link, enter payment details, or download unexpected attachments.

## Scenario 3
- The message uses authority, urgency, secrecy, and financial pressure.
- Contact the manager through a known, trusted phone number or organizational channel.
- Discouraging independent verification is a warning sign.

# Scoring Guide
Award one point for each correctly identified warning sign or safe action. Discuss any incorrect answers and reinforce independent verification and prompt reporting.

# CONCLUSION
Phishing awareness requires careful evaluation of messages, independent verification, and safe reporting not simply judging whether a message looks professional.
