# PHISHING AWARENESS TRAINING

# 1\. Introduction

Phishing is a cyberattack in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person, company, or service to trick someone into revealing sensitive information, opening a malicious attachment, transferring money, or visiting a fraudulent website.
Phishing commonly occurs through email, SMS, social media, phone calls, and messaging applications.

# 2\. Learning Objectives

By the end of this training, participants should be able to:

* Identify common signs of phishing.
* Recognize suspicious links and fake websites.
* Understand social-engineering techniques.
* Apply safe practices when handling unexpected messages.
* Report suspected phishing attempts appropriately.

# 3\. COMMON TYPES OF PHISHING

## Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails impersonate banks, employers, delivery companies, or online services.

## Spear Phishing

A targeted attack customized for a particular person or organization.

## Smishing

Phishing carried out through SMS or text messages.

## Vishing

Phishing conducted through voice calls.

## Clone Phishing

A legitimate-looking message is copied and modified to include a malicious link or attachment.

## Fake Website Phishing

A fraudulent website imitates a trusted website to steal login credentials or payment information.

# 4\. Warning Signs of Phishing

Watch for:

1. Urgent threats or pressure to act immediately.
2. Requests for passwords, PINs, or one-time verification codes.
3. Unexpected attachments or links.
4. Sender addresses that do not match the claimed organization.
5. Misspelled words, unusual wording, or inconsistent branding.
6. Website addresses that imitate legitimate domains.
7. Unexpected requests for money, gift cards, or confidential information.
Note: Professional writing and correct spelling do not guarantee that a message is legitimate.

# 5\. Social Engineering Techniques

Attackers may exploit:

* Fear: "Your account will be suspended."
* Urgency: "Act within 10 minutes."
* Authority: Pretending to be a manager or government official.
* Curiosity: Using surprising attachments or sensational messages.
* Trust: Impersonating a friend, colleague, or familiar organization.
* Reward: Promising prizes, refunds, or unexpected benefits.

# 6\. Example of a Suspicious Email

Subject: URGENT: Account Suspension Warning
Message:
"Dear Customer,
Your account will be suspended today. Verify your password immediately using the link below to restore access.
Example link: https://example.com/
Failure to act may result in permanent account closure."

## Warning signs

* Creates fear and urgency.
* Requests password verification through an unexpected link.
* Uses a generic greeting.
* Provides no independently verified explanation.
The example is for awareness training. The URL is illustrative; do not enter credentials into unfamiliar websites.

# 7\. Best Practices for Prevention

* Verify the sender using a trusted contact method.
* Avoid clicking unexpected links or opening suspicious attachments.
* Visit important services using a saved bookmark or manually entered, verified address.
* Never share passwords or one-time verification codes in response to unexpected requests.
* Enable multifactor authentication (MFA).
* Use updated operating systems, browsers, and security software.
* Report suspicious messages to your organization's IT or security team.
* If you clicked a suspicious link, report it promptly and follow your organization's incident-response procedure.

# 8\. What to Do When You Receive a Phishing Message

1. Stop and assess the message.
2. Do not click links, open attachments, or reply with sensitive information.
3. Verify the request through an independent, trusted channel.
4. Report the message using the approved reporting process.
5. If you disclosed credentials, contact the relevant security team immediately and change affected passwords using a trusted device and website.

# 11\. CONCLUSION

Phishing prevention depends on awareness, verification, and timely reporting. Always pause before acting on unexpected requests, verify through trusted channels, and report suspicious activity promptly.
